# Importance of IP 

## Importance of IP for daily businesses

IP is important in the **knowledge** economy, where most of the costs come from researching the technology and the manufacturing actually is pretty cheap in comparison.

This **increases funding** for innovative projects and gives incentive to inventors to create.

It also helps **small firms** against large firms that could've simply undercut the prices of the small firms due to their access to large production lines and lower manufacturing costs.

IP also allows the creators to **release their inventions** to the public domain as they see fit, be it **Open Source** software, or other types of copyrights licenses (allowing commercial use or only personal use, etc.)

## How patents are used

The main factors for applying for a patent are the following:

- ? Protecting a new **product or process**
	- @ Increasing turnover and profits
	- @ Attracting investors
- ? Licensing
	- @ Allow another firm to use your technology, usually for some price like some part of the profits or royalties
- ? Cross-licensing (whatever that is)
	- @ Licensing between two firms, they each license stuff to each other, it's less of a headache than trying to patent every technology to block competitors
- ? Blocking competitors
- ? Building reputation
- ? To be used in the **future**

## Does IP really make money

For example, in the Music industry, there are only a lucky few that actually get a lot of money from **IP**, think Taylor Swift or others, but otherwise, most of them do not make much money at all from those, even though a lot of money is invested in the protection of the music.

Even if you ask companies about how much of their profit comes from **patents**:

>[!example] Company profits from patents
>![](Pasted%20image%2020260303112412.png)

We see that patents are not the top 1 source of income, but they sometimes are a big part.
Some companies simply prefer to keep everything **secret** and not apply for patents as they deem that those would not be worth it.

Some companies have such a competitive advantage, such as **Nvidia**, that they do not even need to patent their top-end chips, since it would be hard for any other company in the world to be able to reproduce those from a purely technical standpoint.

It seems in this case that the patent was **not effective** since the time to recoup from the costs was way longer than the duration of the patent.
