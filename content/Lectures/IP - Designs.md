# Designs

## Designs - Legal Requirements

>[!properties] Slides
>![](Pasted%20image%2020260331112233.png)

Design refers to the **appearance of the whole or part of the product**, it can refer to *lines, contours, shape, texture, material, ornamentation, combination of material or colors*, it can also refer to **software** with UI and so on.

The design refers to **nothing technical**, it's just a picture of the **appearance of the product**.

>[!example] Examples
>![](Pasted%20image%2020260331112513.png)
>
>The dotted lines on the shoes mean that we don't care about that part, we only want to register our design of the **front part**

The requirements for registering a EU Community Design are as follows:

- ? **Novelty**
	- ![](Pasted%20image%2020260331112627.png)
	- ![](Pasted%20image%2020260331112644.png)
	- Here, *public* means that someone who is well-versed in the specific market domain could know about it. Again, that's the *hypothetical worker* we already talked about in another lecture, someone who worked in the industry and has experience in this market.
- ? **Individual Character**
	- ![](Pasted%20image%2020260331112701.png)
- ! No **absolute grounds for refusal**
	- ![](Pasted%20image%2020260331112733.png)
- ! No **relative grounds for refusal**
	- Conflict with a prior design
	- Or conflict with a prior trademark, geographical indication, or copyrighted work

>>[!note] Differences between registered and unregistered designs

>
>How do we actually *register* for EU Design Directives and EU Community Designs?
>![](Pasted%20image%2020260331114113.png)
>
>For **Switzerland** you register by the **IGE/IPI** and there's **no examination of novelty and individual character**
>
>At the **International level**, you apply through the **WIPO** and you choose which countries you want it to be applied to.
>
>>[!example] Example for EU Community Designs
>>![](Pasted%20image%2020260331114256.png)
>
>To note here is that there is **no opposition**, since it would be too easy to oppose design registrations (that's what he said in the lecture idk), the only thing you can do against it is an **invalidity claim** *after* the registration if:
>
>- The design fails to meet the definition of a design
>- Requirements for protection are not fulfilled
>- Design is excluded from protection
>- Holder is not entitled to the design
>- Design is in conflict with a **prior right**
>- Design uses certain emblems (such as a flag, or the red cross)
