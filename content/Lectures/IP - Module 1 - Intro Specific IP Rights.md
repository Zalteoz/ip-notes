# Patents

>[!note] Definition
>A patent is a **legal title** which grants the holder a few things:
>- The exclusive right to prevent others from making, using or offering for sale, selling or importing a product that infringes his patent without his authorization
>- For every country where the patent was granted
>- **But for a limited time** (up to 20 years)
>
>In return for this protection, the holder of the patent has to **disclose the invention to the public**

This is really important for what we call the **Public Goods Game**:

If those patent laws didn't exist, we would get major issues. For example, if some pharma company decided to invest billions of dollars in **research** to get a new vaccine out to people, once it finally does it, it will need to make a return on investment by having moderately high prices for the vaccine.

The problem with this is that the only difficult part is to find out how to make the vaccine, but then, producing it costs nothing. 

If we didn't have **patent laws** in place, any other company could just *steal* the idea so to speak, and produce the exact same vaccine using the *recipe* that the other company developed.

Since the second company didn't have any costs of research and development, they can sell the vaccine **much cheaper**.

Because of this, in a world without patent law, **no company would be incentivized to make progress**.

This is why we are able to protect inventions that **solve technical problems**:

- ? Chemical substances, drugs
- ? Processes, methods, uses
- ? Products, devices and systems

However, there are some things that are still not *patentable*:
- ! Business methods
- ! Rules of games
- ! Methods of treatment (surgery)
- ! Computer software (that's insane how)

***

## Conditions of protection of patent

>[!properties] Conditions and exclusions
>The patent is protected:
>- Invention (something that didn't exist)
>- New
>- Inventive step
>- Industrial application
>
>It's excluded from protection:
>- Software
>- Discoveries, scientific theories, mathematical methods
>- Aesthetic creations
>- Medical treatment methods
>- ...

If the **patent is given**, the author can then **forbid unauthorized use** of a patented product, a patented process, a product directly obtained from the patented process, and other exceptions, for **up to 20 years** from the patent filing date.

# Copyright

Copyrights protect **literary and artistic** works in a broad shape, this one includes **software**, web sites, photographs, architecture, improvs, etc.

The condition is that the piece must be **original** (the author created it themselves, it's an imprint of the author's personality)

However, **copyrights** only protect the **expression of the idea**, not the idea itself, which is the difference with the patent or trade secret law. 

>[!note] Example of what this means
>This means that if we have a copyright on some movie called *Star Wars*, we can protect the expression of the idea of a *War in the Stars* presented in the movie *Star Wars*, but we can't really protect the whole idea itself, someone can come up and present its own expression of the same idea, for example *Star Trek*.

The duration of the **copyright** is a *lot longer* than that of the patent, and it has a different condition.

Typically the **copyright** is protected up until **70 years after the death of the author**. Naturally, this becomes a bit more complicated when there are multiple authors and such, we've seen that happen with Mickey Mouse, which lost its orignal copyright from the very first animation films it's been in (Steamboat Willie). Here, they decided that the copyright would be lifted 70 years after the death of the **last author** of Steamboat Willie.

However, Disney still has the **trademark** for Mickey Mouse. And there are still lots of restrictions about using it. More specifically, Disney has copyrights on all newer iterations of Mickey Mouse that appeared after Steamboat Willie.

# Trademarks

>[!properties] Definition
>A trademark is any **sign** which **distinguishes** the **goods and services** of one brand from those of another.

There are many different types of trademarks such as **word, figurative, colour patterns, shape**

The requirements for a trademark are:
- ? Capable of **graphic reproduction**
- ? **Distinctive** character
- ? No absolute grounds for refusal

And the function of a trademark is to know the origin of a product, its quality and serve as advertising. Basically it tells you that if you see the Coke logo on a bottle of soda, you're guaranteed to get some product of which you know the quality, the taste, etc.

The duration of a trademark is **10 years** but it can be **renewed** indefinitely, you just need to not forget to do it.

***

# Design rights

>[!properties] Definition
>A design is the **outward appearance** of the whole or parts of a ***product*** resulting from its features

The design can be both two or three dimensional and the requirements are:
- ? Appearance of the product is **new**
- ? **Individual** character
- ? Exception: Form imposed by technical function (you can't say it's design when it has to look this way to function as it needs to)

The duration for those are typically of **5 years** and you can renew them **4 times** maximum.

***

# Know-how, trade secrets

>[!properties] Definition
>Confidential information that has a **commercial or economic value**, for example the **formula for Coke.**
>It must have business, commercial or economic value (actual or potential) because the information is not generally known (the value comes from the fact that it's secret).
>
>There are some efforts to keep it a secret

The duration for those is typically **unlimited**, but it's dealt with on a case-by-case basis, this IP law object is less common than the ones above.
